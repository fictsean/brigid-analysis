# van Oldenborgh et al. 2021 — Attribution of the Australian bushfire risk

**Citation**: van Oldenborgh, G. J., Krikken, F., Lewis, S., Leach, N. J., Lehner, F., Saunders, K. R.,
van Weele, M., Haustein, K., Li, S., Wallom, D., Sparrow, S., Arrighi, J., Singh, R. K., van Aalst,
M. K., Philip, S. Y., Vautard, R., and Otto, F. E. L. (2021). "Attribution of the Australian bushfire
risk to anthropogenic climate change." *Natural Hazards and Earth System Sciences*, 21, 941–960.

**Link**: [https://doi.org/10.5194/nhess-21-941-2021](https://doi.org/10.5194/nhess-21-941-2021)

## Summary

The World Weather Attribution rapid study of the 2019–20 Black Summer bushfire season. Reports
multiple metrics: the **ERA5-based FWI7x-SM** probability increased "more than a factor of 4"; the
Monthly Severity Rating ">9"; the **model-based FWI** only "at least 30%" (PR ≥ 1.3, which WWA flag as
an underestimate because models understate the Australian heat trend); and heat extremes ~10× more
likely (obs) / ≥2× (models). No attributable trend in low precipitation.

## Why it matters here

Our primary external benchmark for Black Summer. Our GEV shift-fit PR=4.0 / FAR=0.752 matches the
**ERA5 FWI7x-SM** lower bound (">4") — the observational metric, not the conservative model-FWI.
