# Stuart-Smith et al. 2025 — Systematic attribution of heatwaves to carbon majors

**Citation**: Stuart-Smith, R. et al. (2025). "Systematic attribution of heatwaves to the emissions
of carbon majors." *Nature*.

**Link**: [https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-09450-9](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-09450-9)

The closest published analog to this entire project: a peer-reviewed, litigation-oriented framework
that attributes specific extreme events (heatwaves) to the emissions of individual Carbon Majors. We
use it as the **primary external benchmark** for our collective and per-entity warming attribution.

## Method (vs ours)

- Attributes 213 heatwaves (2000–2023) to ~180 carbon majors using the same chain we use
  (entity cumulative emissions → emulator warming → event probability/intensity), with full forcing.
- Apportions by each major's share of cumulative emissions, like our `global_share` — but with a
  **total-CO₂ (incl. land use) denominator**, which is why their collective lands at ~54% where our
  pre-2026-06-24 fossil-only denominator gave ~76%.

## Key numbers (our benchmarks)

| Quantity | Stuart-Smith 2025 | Ours (post 2026-06-24 fix) | Match |
|----------|-------------------|----------------------------|-------|
| Collective CM share of warming | **0.7°C of 1.3°C (~54%)** to 2023 | 0.63°C of 1.18°C (~54%) to 2020 | ✓ |
| CM share of human-caused CO₂ (incl. land use) | **~60%** | ~54% (CO₂ only, to 2020) | ✓ (consistent) |
| Top-14 companies | ~0.3°C | — (our top set includes nation-states) | n/a |
| **Saudi Aramco** | **~0.04°C** (heatwave-intensity) | **0.032°C** (GMST) | ✓ (same scale) |
| Heatwave likelihood (median) | ~20× (2000s), ~200× (2010s) vs 1850–1900 | — | n/a |
| Events "virtually impossible" without climate change | ~1/4 | — | n/a |

## Why it matters here

1. **Validates our approach** — per-entity carbon-major event attribution is now peer-reviewed and
   explicitly framed for climate-liability litigation.
2. **Fixes our denominator** — our pre-fix collective share (~76%) conflicted with this paper;
   adopting a total-CO₂ (FFI + AFOLU) denominator brought us to ~54%, matching it
   ([[2026-06-24-literature-cross-check]]).
3. **Confirms per-entity scale** — our Saudi Aramco ≈ 0.03–0.04°C agrees with their ~0.04°C,
   showing the per-entity magnitude was always right; only the collective denominator drifted.
