# Ekwurzel et al. (2017) — Warming and Sea Level Traced to Named Producers

**Full citation**: Ekwurzel, B., Boneham, J., Dalton, M.W., Heede, R., Mera, R.J., Allen, M.R., & Frumhoff, P.C. (2017). "The rise in global atmospheric CO2, surface temperature, and sea level from emissions traced to major carbon producers." *Climatic Change*, 144(4), 579–590. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10584-017-1978-0

## What It Does

Feeds the Carbon Majors emissions database (Heede 2014) into the MAGICC reduced-complexity climate model to attribute observed warming and sea level rise to individual fossil fuel producers. The foundational quantitative precedent for the emissions → warming → liability chain.

## Key Findings

- 90 major carbon producers are responsible for ~57% of industrial CO2 and CH4 emissions since 1880
- These emissions contributed ~0.5°C of observed warming since pre-industrial (out of ~1°C total at time of publication)
- Saudi Aramco, Chevron, ExxonMobil, BP, Shell are consistently in the top tier
- Sea level rise of ~8cm attributable to these same producers

## Relevance to This Project

This paper is the direct methodological ancestor of our emissions-to-warming step. We extend it in three ways:
1. Use FaIR v2.2 instead of MAGICC (more recent, AR6-constrained)
2. Use the 2026 Carbon Majors release (178 entities, updated to 2024)
3. Propagate uncertainty through an 841-member posterior ensemble rather than a single model run

Our top-line finding (Saudi Aramco 44.7 m°C; Carbon Majors ~76% of observed warming, close to the ~71% Heede coverage figure) is consistent with Ekwurzel et al. scaled for the updated dataset and time period.

## Legal Significance

This paper is cited in climate litigation as establishing that individual producers can be assigned quantitative warming contributions. It underpins the scientific credibility of liability arguments in cases such as *Juliana v. United States* and various state-level proceedings.
