# Ekwurzel et al. (2017) — Warming and Sea Level Traced to Named Producers

**Citation**: Ekwurzel, B., Boneham, J., Dalton, M.W., Heede, R., Mera, R.J., Allen, M.R., & Frumhoff, P.C. (2017). "The rise in global atmospheric CO2, surface temperature, and sea level from emissions traced to major carbon producers." *Climatic Change*, 144(4), 579–590.

**Link**: [https://doi.org/10.1007/s10584-017-1978-0](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10584-017-1978-0)

## What It Does

Feeds the Carbon Majors emissions database (Heede 2014) into the MAGICC reduced-complexity climate model to attribute observed warming and sea level rise to individual fossil fuel producers. The foundational quantitative precedent for the emissions → warming → liability chain.

## Key Findings

- 90 major carbon producers are responsible for ~57% of the rise in atmospheric CO2 since 1880, and **~42–50% of the rise in GMST** (≈0.4°C of the ~0.9°C 1880–2010 rise; ~29–35% since 1980)
- The paper reports **no robust single-company °C figure** (per-producer contributions are tens of milli-degrees, not tenths of a degree)
- Saudi Aramco, Chevron, ExxonMobil, BP, Shell are consistently in the top tier
- Sea level rise of ~8cm attributable to these same producers

## Relevance to This Project

This paper is the direct methodological ancestor of our emissions-to-warming step. We extend it in three ways:
1. Use FaIR v2.2 instead of MAGICC (more recent, AR6-constrained)
2. Use the 2026 Carbon Majors release (178 entities, updated to 2024)
3. Propagate uncertainty through an 841-member posterior ensemble rather than a single model run

Our top-line finding (Saudi Aramco ~32 m°C ≈ 0.03°C; Carbon Majors ~54% of observed warming) is
consistent with Ekwurzel et al. (~42–50%) scaled for the updated dataset and time period, and with
the larger [[stuart-smith-2025]] event-attribution study (~54%, Aramco ~0.04°C). **NB**: the ~71%
Heede figure is a share of *emissions*, not warming — an earlier version of this page wrongly used it
to validate our warming share, and cited an erroneous "Aramco ~0.37°C" (off by ~10×); both corrected
2026-06-24 ([[2026-06-24-literature-cross-check]]).

## Legal Significance

This paper is cited in climate litigation as establishing that individual producers can be assigned quantitative warming contributions. It underpins the scientific credibility of liability arguments in cases such as *Juliana v. United States* and various state-level proceedings.
